# Part A — Writing Task 1: Writing an Email (27 minutes, 150–200 words)

# Email 1 — Noisy construction near your apartment

> **A construction company has been working on a building next to your apartment. The work often starts at 6:30 a.m., including weekends. Write an email to the city's bylaw office. In your email:**
> - describe the problem;
> - explain how it is affecting you and your family;
> - say what you would like the city to do.

## CELPIP 10+ model answer

Dear Bylaw Office,

I am writing to report ongoing noise from a construction site at 214 Maple Street, directly beside my apartment building. For the past three weeks, the crew has been starting heavy work, including drilling and the unloading of steel beams, at around 6:30 a.m., and this happens on Saturdays and Sundays as well.

The early noise is having a serious effect on my household. My husband works night shifts at the hospital and usually sleeps until noon, but he is now woken up every morning. Our two young children are also tired and irritable, and my daughter's teacher has mentioned that she is struggling to concentrate in class.

I understand that construction is necessary, but I believe the city's noise bylaw does not allow this kind of work so early, especially on weekends. I would be grateful if an officer could visit the site, confirm the start times, and remind the company of the permitted hours.

Thank you for your attention to this matter. I can be reached at 604-555-0182 if you need more information.

Sincerely,
Priya Nair

**Notes (CELPIP 10+):**

- **Content/Coherence:** all three points are covered with precise details (the address, the times, the types of noise, who is affected).
- **Vocabulary:** precise and natural (*ongoing*, *irritable*, *permitted hours*, *remind the company*).
- **Readability:** clear paragraphs, varied sentences and no errors.
- **Task Fulfillment:** a formal, polite tone that suits a city office; the request is specific.

*(The address, phone number and name are examples. The 555 number is a fictional format.)*

## CELPIP 8 model answer

My children are also tired, because they cannot sleep well. My daughter's teacher said that she is not concentrating at school. I know that the building has to be finished, but I think it is not allowed to start so early, especially on weekends. Could you please send someone to check the site and ask the company to start later? I would really appreciate your help.

- **Content/Coherence:** complete and well organized; fewer details.
- **Vocabulary:** adequate, mostly common words.
- **Readability:** accurate and clear.
- **Task Fulfillment:** an appropriate tone and request.

# Email 2 — Thanking a colleague

> **A colleague helped you a lot during your first month at a new job. You are now moving to another team. Write an email to your colleague. In your email:**
> - thank them and mention specific ways they helped;
> - explain your new role;
> - suggest how you could stay in touch.

## CELPIP 10+ model answer

Hi Marco,

As you may have heard, I'm moving to the Logistics team next Monday, and I didn't want to leave without properly thanking you for everything you've done for me this past month.

When I started, I felt completely lost with our inventory software, but you patiently walked me through it, even staying late on my second day to explain the reporting features. You also introduced me to people in other departments, which made it much easier to settle in. Most of all, I appreciated how you always made time for my questions, no matter how busy you were.

In my new role, I'll be coordinating deliveries between our three warehouses, so I'll be working mostly with suppliers and drivers. It's an exciting step, although I'm sure I'll miss our team's Friday lunches.

Since we'll still be in the same building, I'd love to keep having coffee together now and then. How about the second Tuesday of every month? Let me know if that works for you.

Thanks again for everything,
Aiko

**Notes (CELPIP 10+):**

- **Content/Coherence:** three specific examples of help, a clear new role and a concrete plan to stay in touch.
- **Vocabulary:** natural and warm (*walked me through it*, *settle in*, *now and then*).
- **Readability:** fluent, with a good mix of sentence types.
- **Task Fulfillment:** a friendly semiformal tone that suits a colleague.

## CELPIP 8 model answer

Hi Marco,

I want to say a big thank you for your help during my first month here. Next week, I'm moving to the Logistics team, so I won't work with you every day anymore.

You helped me a lot when I started. You showed me how to use the inventory software, and you explained everything slowly and clearly. You also introduced me to people in other departments. Whenever I had a question, you were always ready to help me, even when you were busy with your own work.

In my new job, I will organize deliveries between our warehouses. I will talk a lot with suppliers and drivers. I'm excited, but I'll miss working with you and the team, especially our Friday lunches.

We are still in the same building, so maybe we can have coffee together sometimes. What about once a month? Let me know what you think.

Thanks again,
Aiko

**Notes (CELPIP 8):**

- **Content/Coherence:** all points are covered with some detail.
- **Vocabulary:** suitable but simpler.
- **Readability:** accurate, with some repetition (*You … You … You*).
- **Task Fulfillment:** an appropriate tone.

# Email 3 — Problem with an online order

> **You ordered a winter jacket online, but you received the wrong size and colour. You need the jacket for a trip in two weeks. Write an email to the company's customer service department. In your email:**
> - give details of your order;
> - explain what is wrong;
> - say what you want the company to do.

## CELPIP 10+ model answer

Dear Customer Service Team,

I am writing about order number WJ-48219, which I placed on your website on October 28. I ordered a men's insulated winter jacket in navy blue, size large, and paid $189.99, including shipping.

The parcel arrived yesterday, but unfortunately the jacket inside is a medium in bright red. I have checked my order confirmation, and it clearly shows navy blue in size large, so it seems there was a mistake during packing. The jacket is still in its original packaging with all the tags attached.

I am travelling to Banff in two weeks, and the main reason I bought this jacket was for that trip. I would therefore like you to send the correct jacket by express delivery as soon as possible, and to provide a prepaid return label for the wrong one. If the correct item is not available, I would prefer a full refund so that I can buy one locally before I leave.

I look forward to your prompt reply.

Sincerely,
Daniel Okoro

**Notes (CELPIP 10+):**

- **Content/Coherence:** full order details, a clear problem, a reason for urgency and a request with a backup option.
- **Vocabulary:** precise (*insulated*, *original packaging*, *prepaid return label*, *prompt reply*).
- **Readability:** clear, logical paragraphs; no errors.
- **Task Fulfillment:** formal and firm but polite.

*(The order number and details are examples.)*

# Part B — Writing Task 2: Responding to Survey Questions (26 minutes, 150–200 words)

*Task: read the survey question, choose one option, and explain your choice with reasons. You may mention the other option, but your choice must be clear.*

# Survey 1 — Community centre funding

> **Your city has money to improve one community facility. Which option do you prefer? Explain your reasons.**
> - **Option A:** Build a new indoor swimming pool at the community centre.
> - **Option B:** Expand the public library with more study rooms and computers.

## CELPIP 10+ model answer

I would prefer Option B, expanding the public library, because it would benefit a wider range of residents throughout the year.

First of all, a modern library serves people of every age and background. Students need quiet places to study, especially those who share small apartments with their families. Newcomers to Canada often rely on library computers to search for jobs, complete government forms and take free language courses online. Seniors also use libraries to stay socially active through book clubs and workshops. More study rooms and computers would directly support all of these groups.

Secondly, the library is far more affordable to run than a swimming pool. A pool requires lifeguards, heating, water treatment and regular repairs, which would place a long-term burden on the city's budget. By contrast, additional computers and rooms would have relatively low maintenance costs.

Although a pool would certainly be popular and would encourage exercise, many residents already have access to outdoor pools in summer and to private gyms. For these reasons, I strongly believe that investing in the library would bring greater value to our community.

**Notes (CELPIP 10+):**

- **Content/Coherence:** a clear choice, two well-developed reasons, and the other option acknowledged.
- **Vocabulary:** *wider range*, *rely on*, *long-term burden*, *by contrast*, *greater value*.
- **Readability:** strong paragraphing and linking; no errors.
- **Task Fulfillment:** fully answers the survey.

## CELPIP 8 model answer

I prefer Option B, which is to expand the public library.

The main reason is that many different people use the library. Students go there to study and do their homework, because their homes can be noisy. People who are looking for a job can use the computers to write their résumés and apply online. Older people also visit the library to read newspapers and join activities. If there are more study rooms and computers, more people can use these services.

Another reason is cost. A swimming pool is very expensive to build and to maintain. The city would need to pay lifeguards and heat the water every day. The library would be cheaper in the long term.

I agree that a swimming pool is good for people's health, and children would enjoy it. However, I think the library is more useful for the whole community, so I choose Option B.

**Notes (CELPIP 8):**

- **Content/Coherence:** a clear choice with relevant reasons.
- **Vocabulary:** adequate (*maintain*, *in the long term*).
- **Readability:** accurate and clear.
- **Task Fulfillment:** complete.

**Notes (CELPIP 5):**

# Survey 2 — Work schedule

> **Your company is considering a new work schedule. Which option do you prefer? Explain your reasons.**
> - **Option A:** Work four 10-hour days per week.
> - **Option B:** Work five 8-hour days per week, with the option to work from home on Fridays.

## CELPIP 10+ model answer

Personally, I would choose Option B, five eight-hour days with the possibility of working from home on Fridays.

My main reason is that ten-hour days would be exhausting for many employees, myself included. By the end of a long shift, concentration naturally drops, which could lead to more mistakes, particularly in tasks that require accuracy, such as preparing invoices or checking safety reports. An eight-hour day keeps people more focused and productive.

In addition, Option B fits better with family responsibilities. Parents need to pick up children from school or daycare, and a ten-hour day would make this very difficult. Working from home on Fridays would also save commuting time and allow employees to handle personal matters, such as medical appointments, without taking a full day off.

I can see why a three-day weekend is attractive, and it might suit younger employees without children. However, for the majority of staff, Option B offers a healthier balance between work and personal life, and I believe it would keep productivity high throughout the week.

**Notes (CELPIP 10+):**

- **Content/Coherence:** a clear choice, two strong reasons with examples, and a fair look at the other option.
- **Vocabulary:** *exhausting*, *concentration naturally drops*, *handle personal matters*, *the majority of staff*.
- **Readability:** fluent and error-free.
- **Task Fulfillment:** complete.

## CELPIP 8 model answer

I prefer Option B, working five eight-hour days with the choice to work from home on Fridays.

First, I think ten hours a day is too long. Many workers would feel very tired at the end of the day, and they might make more mistakes. With eight-hour days, people can stay more focused and do better work.

Second, Option B is better for people with families. Many employees have to take their children to school or pick them up in the afternoon. A ten-hour day would make this difficult. Working from home on Fridays would also save time and money on travel, and people could go to appointments without taking a day off.

I understand that some people would like a long weekend with Option A. However, I believe Option B is better for most employees because it gives them a good balance between work and family life, and it helps them do their best work every day.

**Notes (CELPIP 8):**

- **Content/Coherence:** clear and organized.
- **Vocabulary:** adequate.
- **Readability:** accurate.
- **Task Fulfillment:** complete.

# Part C — Speaking Tasks 1–8 (model responses)

*Each task shows the prompt, the timing, and a level 10+ model response. Spoken answers are written as they would be said, with contractions.*

# Task 1 — Giving Advice (30 seconds to prepare, 90 seconds to speak)

> **Your friend has just moved to Canada and wants to find a job in his field, accounting. Give him advice about how to start.**

**Level 10+ model (about 185 words):**

Hey Ali, congratulations on the move! I know finding a job here can feel overwhelming at first, so here are a few things that really helped people I know.

First, I'd get your education assessed as soon as possible. Many employers want to know how your degree compares to a Canadian one, and some professional bodies for accountants also need it if you want to work toward a Canadian designation later.

Second, adapt your résumé to the Canadian style. Keep it to two pages, focus on achievements rather than duties, and don't include a photo or your date of birth.

Third, start networking right away. A lot of jobs here are filled through connections, so join LinkedIn groups for accountants, go to settlement agency workshops, and consider volunteering, maybe doing the books for a local non-profit. That gives you Canadian experience and references.

And finally, be open to a starting position, like a bookkeeper or an accounts assistant. It's a great way to get your foot in the door.

If you like, we can go through your résumé together this weekend.

**Notes:** a friendly opening, four organized pieces of advice with reasons, and a supportive close. Natural spoken linking (*First*, *Second*, *And finally*).

**Level 7 sample (about 95 words):**

Hi Ali, welcome to Canada. I have some advice for you. First, you should make a Canadian résumé, because it is different from your country. Don't put your photo, and write about your achievements. Second, you can use LinkedIn and go to job fairs to meet people. In Canada, many people find jobs by networking. Also, you can volunteer, because it gives you Canadian experience. Maybe you need to start with a small job first, like a bookkeeper, and then you can find a better job later. I hope this helps you. Good luck!

**Notes (Level 7):** the advice is relevant and clearly ordered, but the reasons are shorter, the language is simpler, and there is some repetition (*you can … you can …*).

# Task 2 — Talking About a Personal Experience (30 seconds / 60 seconds)

> **Talk about a time when you had to learn something new quickly. What happened, and how did you manage?**

**Level 10+ model (about 135 words):**

A couple of years ago, my manager suddenly asked me to cover for a colleague who'd gone on medical leave, and part of her job was running our weekly payroll. I'd never done payroll before, and I had just three days to learn it.

On the first day, I sat with her replacement notes and the software manual, and I wrote my own step-by-step checklist. Then I asked our finance director if I could practise on last month's data, so I could compare my results with the real ones and spot any mistakes.

The first real payroll was stressful, I won't lie, but it went through without a single error. Looking back, the experience taught me that breaking a big task into small steps, and not being afraid to ask for help, makes almost anything manageable.

**Notes:** a clear situation, actions in order, the result and a reflection, all within 60 seconds.

# Task 3 — Describing a Scene (30 seconds / 60 seconds)

> **Describe a picture of a busy farmers' market on a sunny Saturday morning.** *(Scene for the image: a street market with fruit and vegetable stalls; a family with a stroller; an older man buying flowers; a musician playing guitar; a child eating ice cream; a "Fresh Bread" sign.)*

**Level 10+ model (about 135 words):**

This picture shows a lively farmers' market on what looks like a sunny Saturday morning. The street is lined with stalls covered in colourful fruit and vegetables, like red apples, bright orange pumpkins and baskets of green beans.

In the centre of the picture, a young family is pushing a stroller, and the mother seems to be pointing at a stall with a big "Fresh Bread" sign. On the right, an older man is paying for a bunch of yellow flowers, and the vendor is smiling as she wraps them up.

Near the front, there's a little boy holding an ice cream cone that's starting to melt, and he looks absolutely delighted. In the background, a musician with a guitar is playing for a small crowd. Overall, the atmosphere seems relaxed, friendly and full of energy.

**Notes:** a general overview first, then organized description by location (*In the centre*, *On the right*, *Near the front*, *In the background*), with vivid but accurate details.

# Task 4 — Making Predictions (30 seconds / 60 seconds)

> **Look at the same farmers' market picture. What do you think will probably happen next?**

**Level 10+ model (about 120 words):**

I think a few things are likely to happen next. First, the little boy's ice cream is already melting, so it'll probably drip onto his shirt, and his parents may have to clean him up with a napkin.

The family with the stroller will probably buy a loaf of bread and maybe some fruit, and then they might stop to listen to the musician for a while. The crowd around him seems to be growing, so I expect some people will drop coins into his guitar case.

As for the older man, he'll most likely take the flowers home as a gift for someone, perhaps his wife. And as the morning goes on, the market will get even busier, especially around lunchtime.

**Notes:** several predictions linked to details in the picture, using a range of future forms (*will probably*, *may*, *might*, *is likely to*, *I expect*).

# Task 5 — Comparing and Persuading (60 seconds to choose, 60 seconds to prepare, 60 seconds to speak)

> **Your family is choosing a summer vacation. You prefer a camping trip to a national park. Your partner prefers a city hotel package. Persuade your partner to choose the camping trip.** *(Details: camping is $600 for a week and includes hiking and lake activities; the hotel is $1,400 for a week and is close to museums and restaurants.)*

**Level 10+ model (about 140 words):**

I know the city hotel sounds comfortable, but I really think the camping trip would be a better choice for us this summer.

First of all, it's much more affordable. The camping trip costs six hundred dollars for the whole week, while the hotel package is fourteen hundred. That's eight hundred dollars we could save, or even put toward a new laptop for the kids.

Second, the kids spend so much time indoors on screens during the school year. A week of hiking and swimming in the lake would get them outside and active, and they'd probably remember it far longer than a museum visit.

And honestly, I think we need a real break from the noise of the city. Waking up by a quiet lake sounds like exactly what we need. So, shall we book the campsite?

**Notes:** a clear, persuasive structure that compares both options using the given details (*six hundred* against *fourteen hundred*), and ends with a call to action.

# Task 6 — Dealing with a Difficult Situation (60 seconds / 60 seconds)

> **You promised to help your friend move to a new apartment on Saturday. However, your manager has just asked you to work that day because a colleague is sick. Choose one: call your friend and explain, or talk to your manager and explain why you can't work.** *(Model: talking to the friend.)*

**Level 10+ model (about 130 words):**

Hi Sara, it's me. I'm really sorry to do this, but I have some bad news about Saturday. My manager just called. One of my coworkers is sick, and there's no one else who can cover the shift, so I have to work. I tried to get out of it, but it's a small team, and I couldn't say no.

I feel awful, because I know you were counting on me. But I don't want to leave you without help. I can come over on Friday evening after work and help you pack all the boxes, so Saturday goes faster. And my brother has a truck. I've already asked him, and he's happy to help you on Saturday morning.

Would that work for you? Again, I'm so sorry.

**Notes:** an apology, a clear reason, empathy, and practical solutions. The tone is suitable for a close friend.

# Task 7 — Expressing Opinions (30 seconds / 90 seconds)

> **Should elementary schools require students to learn a musical instrument? Give your opinion and explain your reasons.**

**Level 10+ model (about 180 words):**

In my opinion, elementary schools should give every student the chance to learn an instrument, but I don't think it should be strictly required.

On the one hand, there are clear benefits. Learning an instrument teaches children patience and discipline, because they have to practise regularly to improve. Research has also linked music education with better memory and concentration. And playing in a school band or orchestra teaches teamwork in a way that few other subjects do.

On the other hand, making it compulsory could cause problems. Instruments can be expensive, and not every family can afford one. Some children simply aren't interested in music, and forcing them might make them dislike it, especially if they're struggling academically.

So I think the best approach is for schools to offer free introductory music classes and lend instruments to students who want them. Children could try a few different instruments for a semester, and those who enjoy it could continue. That way, every child gets the opportunity without the pressure. Overall, encouragement works better than obligation, especially at that age.

**Notes:** a clear, balanced opinion with reasons on both sides and a practical solution, organized for 90 seconds.

# Task 8 — Describing an Unusual Situation (30 seconds / 60 seconds)

> **You see an unusual object in a store and want to describe it to a friend on the phone, who might want it as a gift. The object is a coffee mug shaped like a cactus, with a lid that looks like a pink flower.**

**Level 10+ model (about 125 words):**

Hey Tom, I'm at the gift shop downtown, and I've just found something perfect for your sister. It's a coffee mug, but it's shaped like a cactus. It's about as tall as a normal mug, maybe a bit taller, and it's bright green, with tiny white dots that look like the cactus spines, although they're smooth, so you won't hurt your hands.

The best part is the lid. It's shaped like a pink flower with five petals, and you lift it off by holding the yellow centre. It's made of ceramic, and the label says it's safe for the dishwasher.

It costs twenty-four dollars. Since she loves plants, I think she'd find it really funny. Do you want me to buy it for you?

**Notes:** a clear description of shape, size, colour, material and function, with a comparison to a normal mug, a practical detail (dishwasher-safe) and a question to the friend.
