## CELPIP Writing Task 2 (Survey): there is no right answer

Task 2 puts a situation in front of you — usually a community or workplace decision that people
disagree about — and offers **two options**. It asks which you prefer and why. Paragon's own sample
instruction is short: *choose the option you prefer, explain the reasons for your choice, and write
**"about 150-200 words."***

Here is what trips people up. **Neither option is correct.** There is no answer key. The marker is
not checking whether you agreed with them. They are checking **how well you defended the side you
picked** — and whether you actually picked one.

## The commonest way to fail this task

Test-takers who write well in every other section still lose marks here, and it is almost always the
same instinct: **balance**.

You have been taught, sensibly, that a good argument acknowledges the other side. So you write
*"both options have advantages"*, give two lines to each, and close with *"it depends on the
situation."*

That is a thoughtful paragraph. It is also **a survey response that did not respond to the survey.**

And it runs straight into CELPIP's harshest rule:

> A response giving **insufficient information to assess** scores **0 or 1**.

If the marker cannot tell which option you chose, there is nothing to assess. Not a low score — the
bottom of the scale, from an essay that may contain excellent English.

**Choose in the first sentence. Then spend the rest of the response earning it.**

## A worked example

**The prompt** *(original AlmiWorld practice prompt — same shape as the real task)*

> Your community centre has money to improve one thing this year. Members have been asked which they
> would prefer.
>
> **Option A — Extend the opening hours.** The centre would open two hours earlier on weekdays.
> **Option B — Renovate the main hall.** The hall would get new flooring, lighting and seating.
>
> Choose the option you prefer and explain your reasons. Write about 150–200 words.

**The response**

> I would prefer Option A, extending the opening hours.
>
> The main reason is who it reaches. The centre currently opens at nine, which means anyone starting
> work at eight cannot use it before their shift. Opening at seven would put the gym and the pool
> within reach of shift workers, parents doing a school run, and people who simply cannot come in the
> evening. That is a group the centre does not serve at all at the moment.
>
> The hall does need work, and I am not pretending otherwise. But it is used for perhaps two events a
> month, and it is usable now — the seating is tired, not broken. Longer hours would be felt every
> weekday by people who currently walk past a locked door.
>
> There is also the cost. Renovations are spent once and then they age. Opening hours are a
> commitment the centre can review each year, and drop if the early slots turn out to be empty.
>
> For those reasons I would put this year's money into hours rather than flooring.

**Why this works.**

- **The choice is in sentence one.** A marker knows within four words what is being defended.
- **Three distinct reasons, not one reason three times** — who it reaches, how often each option is
  used, and how the money behaves over time.
- **The other option is acknowledged, then defeated.** *"The hall does need work, and I am not
  pretending otherwise"* is not fence-sitting — it concedes and then explains why the concession does
  not change the answer. That is what a strong argument does, and it is the opposite of
  *"it depends."*
- **Specifics carry it.** Opens at nine, starting work at eight, two events a month, seating tired
  not broken. Every one of those is invented — and that is allowed. **You are not being fact-checked.
  You are being read for how you argue.**

Length: 197 words.

## Three habits that lift this task

1. **Pick the side that is easier to argue, not the side you believe.** You are not being asked for
   your soul. You are being asked to build a case in 200 words.
2. **Reasons must be different from each other.** Three angles on the same reason reads as one thin
   reason padded out.
3. **Invent freely, and be concrete.** Names, hours, numbers, small details. The marker is not
   verifying the community centre exists.
