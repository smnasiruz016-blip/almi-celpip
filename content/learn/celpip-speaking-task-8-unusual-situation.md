## The last task, and the one people prepare for least

Task 8 shows you an image of something **unusual** — an object that should not exist, a scene that
does not quite make sense, a thing being used for the wrong purpose — and asks you to describe it to
a person **who cannot see it.**

Both halves of that sentence are the task. Most answers lose marks on one of them.

## Half one: the listener is blind

Task 3 also asks you to describe an image, and the phone-call reframe fixes it. Task 8 **writes that
reframe into the prompt itself.** Somebody is on the other end with nothing but your voice.

Which means these sentences do no work at all:

> *"As you can see, the thing on the left is quite strange."*
> *"It is like this, and the other part is like that."*
> *"There is a sort of round object here."*

*Here. This. That. As you can see.* Every one of them assumes a shared picture. Your listener has
none. Delete them from this task entirely.

## Half two: the thing is strange, and you must say how

The image is chosen to be odd. The marker wants to hear you **name the oddness** — not tiptoe around
it.

Weak:

> *"There is a chair and it has some things on it. The chair looks different from a normal chair."*

Strong:

> *"It's a dining chair, except somebody has replaced all four legs with stacked books — three tall
> piles of hardbacks and one pile that's clearly shorter, so the whole thing leans."*

The second one describes the same object and actually tells you what is wrong with it.

## The move that carries this task: compare it to a normal thing

You do not have vocabulary for an object that does not exist. **Nobody does.** So describe it against
something the listener already knows:

- *"It's shaped like a kettle, but the spout comes out of the top instead of the side."*
- *"Imagine a bicycle where both wheels are the same size as a coin."*
- *"It's about the size of a microwave, and roughly that shape, but it's made of what looks like woven
  rope."*

**Size, shape, material, and what it resembles.** Four handles, and they work on anything.

## The shape that works

1. **One sentence: what is it closest to?** *"It looks like a phone box, but…"*
2. **Say what is wrong with it.** The single strangest thing, first.
3. **Give scale and material.** How big, made of what.
4. **Describe the parts in order** — top to bottom, or left to right. Pick one and hold it. A listener
   who cannot see needs a route through the object, not a scatter of details.
5. **Say what a person in the image is doing about it**, if there is one. Reaction tells the listener
   more than another adjective.
6. **Guess what it is for.** *"My best guess is that it's meant to…"* You are allowed to be wrong.

## A worked example

**The image** *(described here for reading; in the test you would see it)*

> A man in a suit sits at a bus stop. Where the bench should be, there is a full-sized bathtub,
> plumbed to nothing, resting on the pavement. He is reading a newspaper inside it.

**The response** *(spoken, about 60 seconds)*

> Picture a normal city bus stop — glass shelter, timetable on the wall, road in front. Everything is
> ordinary except the seating.
>
> Instead of a bench, there's a full-sized white bathtub sitting on the pavement, the old kind with
> feet. It isn't connected to anything; there's no plumbing, it's just standing there under the
> shelter, taking up the whole width of it.
>
> A man in a grey suit is sitting inside the tub — not on the edge, *in* it, with his knees up and his
> briefcase balanced on the rim. He's reading a newspaper and he looks completely relaxed, as though
> this is where he always waits.
>
> Nobody else in the photo is looking at him, which somehow makes it stranger.
>
> My guess is it's either an advert for a bathroom company, or one of those art installations councils
> put up. Either way, he's decided it's a seat.

**Why this works.**

- **Sentence one gives the listener a frame** to hang everything on: an ordinary bus stop.
- **The oddness is named immediately**, and precisely — a bathtub, unplumbed, under the shelter.
- **Scale and material are there**: full-sized, white, the kind with feet.
- **The correction is the best line in the answer.** *"Not on the edge, in it."* That is a speaker who
  can hear their own ambiguity and fix it — exactly what "easy to follow" means.
- **It ends with a guess**, offered as a guess.

## What is not being marked

- **Not whether you identify the object correctly.** There often is no correct answer.
- **Not your accent.**
- **Not unusual vocabulary.** Plain words used precisely beat rare words used vaguely. *"About the
  size of a microwave"* is worth more than *"of considerable dimensions."*

And the rule underneath everything:

> A response giving **insufficient information to assess** scores **0 or 1**.

Sixty seconds of *"it's very strange, it's not normal, it's a bit unusual"* reaches that. Strange is
your reaction. **How** it is strange is the answer.

## The habit that fixes this task

Find an object in the room and describe it for forty seconds **to someone who cannot see it** — no
pointing, no *"this"*, no *"here"*. Then, if you can, have somebody draw it from your description
alone. Whatever they get wrong is exactly what your Task 8 answer is missing.
