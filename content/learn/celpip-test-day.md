**Two official rules do most of the damage on test day, and both are about the clock and the door.**
You must arrive **45 minutes** before your test, and check-in **closes 15 minutes before it starts**.
Paragon's wording on what happens if you miss that is not soft:

> "Latecomers will be denied entrance to the test and will NOT be eligible for a refund"

The second rule is shorter still:

> "Your identification is the only personal belonging allowed in the test room."

Not your phone. Not your water bottle. Not the notes you made on the bus.

## The failure

The failure is not a language failure. It is arriving at what you think is a sensible time — ten
minutes early, the way you would for a dentist — and finding check-in already shut. **The door
closes 15 minutes before your test time**, not at it.

The second failure is quieter: turning up with an ID that is the wrong *kind* of document for the
test you booked, or a name on it that does not match your registration. The accepted list is not one
list — it changes depending on which CELPIP test you are sitting and why.

## The arrival timeline

| When | What is happening |
|---|---|
| 45 minutes before your test time | You must arrive. This is the official instruction, not a suggestion. |
| Between then and 15 minutes before | Check-in. Have your ID in hand. |
| 15 minutes before your test time | **Check-in closes.** |
| After that | Latecomers are denied entrance, with no refund eligibility. |

Build your morning backwards from the 45-minute mark. If your test is at 9:00, your deadline is
8:15 — and the point after which you have lost the fee is 8:45.

## The ID rules — the part worth reading twice

Whatever you bring must be **original, valid and unexpired**, and it must match the registration
details you entered during registration, **including full name and document number**. A photocopy
of a passport is not a passport. A renewal receipt is not a document. A middle name you left off the
booking form is a mismatch.

Now the part almost nobody lays out: **the accepted list is different for each test and purpose.**

**CELPIP-General, taken for immigration purposes:**

| Accepted |
|---|
| Passport booklet |
| United States Passport Card |
| Refugee protection claimant document |
| Travel document |

**CELPIP-General LS:**

| Accepted |
|---|
| Passport booklet |
| United States Passport Card |
| Canadian permanent resident card |
| Travel document |

**For non-IRCC purposes**, the list additionally includes:

| Also accepted |
|---|
| Canadian Provincial identification card |
| Canadian Provincial Driver's licence |

Read those three blocks against each other. A **Canadian permanent resident card** is on the LS list
but not on the CELPIP-General immigration list. A **provincial driver's licence** appears only for
non-IRCC purposes. Whatever your friend brought is not evidence about your test — check the list for
*your* test and *your* purpose on Paragon's
[test day page](https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/test-day-information/) before you leave.

## What the room is actually like

The test is **"fully computer delivered. However, the test must be completed in person at one of our
test centres."** **There is no at-home option** — if a page or an agent offers you one, that is not
CELPIP.

What you get at the workstation:

- A **standard QWERTY English keyboard**. If you normally type on a different layout, that is worth
  practising before test day, not discovering on it.
- On-screen **personal timer**, **word counter** and **spell-check**.
- **Paper and pen are provided** for notes. You do not bring your own, and you would not be allowed
  to.

And the shape of the day: **the whole test is done in one sitting.** CELPIP-General runs under 3
hours — the format page puts it more precisely at **under 2 hours and 50 minutes**. CELPIP-General LS
takes **approximately 70 minutes**, also in one sitting. There is no coming back tomorrow for the
half you did not finish.

## What is NOT officially published — so this page will not invent it

Every competing test-day page states these confidently. None of them says where the number came from.
Here is what we could not find on an official Paragon or IRCC source, and therefore will not assert:

- **Refund and rescheduling amounts.** The one fee we can source is the **test transfer fee of CAD
  50 + tax**. Anything beyond that — cancellation refunds, partial refunds, no-show policies with
  dollar figures — is not published where we could verify it.
- **Accommodation arrangements.** How to request them, deadlines, what is granted: not documented in
  the official sources we checked. Contact Paragon directly rather than trusting a forum post.
- **Age rules.** No official minimum or maximum age was found. We are not going to guess one.
- **Whether results timing differs by country.** The published 2–4 business days is not qualified by
  region on the results page, and we found nothing that says it varies. That is an absence of
  evidence, not evidence of sameness.

Fees, for context: as published on celpip.ca on **24 August 2026**, CELPIP-General is **CAD 290 +
tax** and CELPIP-General LS is **CAD 195 + tax** on the Ontario region page. Fees differ by country.
**Check the current fee for your region before you register** — no newer fee-change notice was found,
so we cannot confirm those figures are unchanged today.

## Results

Your results are **"available online through your CELPIP Account in 2-4 business days after your test
date"**, per the [test results page](https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/test-results/). Other figures
circulate, including on other CELPIP pages — the results page is the one this site cites.

Two things about the report itself. **The PDF score report is the official document** — it is the
version institutions accept, not a screenshot of your account dashboard. And it stays viewable in
your account **for two years from your test date**. That is Paragon's account-access window and it is
a separate thing from any immigration programme's rule about how old a result may be. Do not treat
one as the other, and check IRCC's own page for the rule that applies to your application.

## What actually costs you

- Arriving at your test time instead of 45 minutes before it. The door shuts at minus 15.
- Bringing an ID that is valid but not on the list for *your* test and purpose.
- A name or document number on your ID that does not match what you typed at registration.
- Bringing anything else into the room — phone, bottle, notes. Only your identification goes in.
- Assuming a home option exists. It does not; the test is in person at a test centre.
- Planning around a refund policy nobody has published. Assume a missed check-in costs you the fee.
