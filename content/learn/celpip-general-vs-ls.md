**If you book the LS test for permanent residence, you have bought the wrong exam.** IRCC's own
bulletin is unambiguous: CELPIP-General is accepted for immigration and citizenship;
CELPIP-General LS is accepted for citizenship purposes only. The Express Entry page says it flatly —
"You must take the CELPIP-General test."

That is the version everyone eventually hears about, usually too late.

**The half nobody writes about is that the mistake also runs the other way.** Someone applying for
citizenship needs Listening and Speaking at CLB 4 and nothing else. Book the full General test
anyway and you sit roughly three hours instead of about seventy minutes, pay CAD 290 instead of
CAD 195, and spend weeks studying Reading and Writing sections you will never see.

## The failure

The two tests share a name, a score scale and a booking page. The difference between them appears as
a one-line FAQ on a couple of score-chart pages and almost nowhere else — so the failure is not that
people ignore the distinction, it is that they never meet it while choosing.

- You need PR, you book LS because it is cheaper and shorter. **IRCC will not accept it for an
  immigration application.**
- You need citizenship, you book General because it is the one everyone talks about. Accepted — just
  longer, dearer, and half of what you studied was never required of you.

Nothing on this page tells you which to book. That depends on the program you are applying to, and
IRCC's page for that program is the only thing that decides it.

## Side by side

| | CELPIP-General | CELPIP-General LS |
|---|---|---|
| Components | Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking | Listening and Speaking only |
| Total time | Under 2 hours 50 minutes, one sitting | Approximately 70 minutes, one sitting |
| Fee (Ontario page, 24 Aug 2026) | CAD 290 + tax | CAD 195 + tax |
| Accepted by IRCC for | Immigration **and** citizenship | Citizenship purposes **only** |
| Elsewhere | Also designated by Australia's Department of Home Affairs for visa purposes | — |

Both are fully computer delivered and both must be completed in person at a test centre. There is no
at-home option for either.

The accepted ID lists also differ. For CELPIP-General taken for immigration purposes: passport
booklet, United States Passport Card, refugee protection claimant document, travel document. For
CELPIP-General LS: passport booklet, United States Passport Card, **Canadian permanent resident
card**, travel document. Either way your ID must be original, valid and unexpired, and must match
the registration details you entered, including full name and document number.

## What IRCC says, in IRCC's words

Paragon describes its own products, but it does not decide what an application may contain. IRCC
does. The operational bulletin puts it in two sentences:

> "The CELPIP-General (CELPIP-G) test results are accepted by IRCC for both immigration and
> citizenship purposes"

> "the CELPIP-General ‒ Listening and Speaking (CELPIP-G LS) test is accepted by IRCC for citizenship
> purposes only"

And the Express Entry language testing page leaves no room to interpret:

> "You must take the CELPIP-General test"

Check both pages yourself before booking. They carry date-modified stamps — the bulletin 2026-06-23,
the Express Entry page 2026-06-22 — and government pages change.

## The fee and the clock

As published on celpip.ca on **24 August 2026** (Ontario region page): CELPIP-General is
**CAD 290 + tax**, CELPIP-General LS is **CAD 195 + tax**. A test transfer is CAD 50 + tax.

**Fees differ by country** — Paragon's 2024 fee-change notice lists figures in Mexican pesos,
Japanese yen and Brazilian reais. No newer notice was found, so treat the CAD numbers as published
on the date checked, not as today's guaranteed price. Open the region page for where you will sit.

The time gap is underestimated more often than the fee. Under 2 hours 50 minutes versus about 70
minutes is not a longer morning — it is two extra components, Reading at 43–56 minutes and
38 questions, Writing at 53 minutes and two tasks.

## What LS actually contains

If LS is the test you need, this is the whole scope of your preparation.

**Listening — 46 to 55 minutes, 38 questions, six parts:**

| Part | Questions |
|---|---|
| 1. Problem Solving | 8 |
| 2. Daily Life Conversation | 5 |
| 3. Listening for Information | 6 |
| 4. News Item | 5 |
| 5. Discussion | 8 |
| 6. Viewpoints | 6 |

**All audio clips are played one time only.** Paper and pen are provided. You *read* the answer
options — they are not spoken — so the load is split between listening and reading on screen. Part 5
is a video with three speakers. Part 6 is one speaker for about three minutes. You cannot return to
a previous part once you have moved on.

**Speaking — 15 minutes, 8 tasks.** Each task runs under two minutes including preparation, which is
the defining constraint: there is no room to plan at length. One official rule matters more here
than any technique — a response giving insufficient information to assess scores 0 or 1.

That is the whole syllabus. No Reading. No Writing.

## Citizenship: CLB 4, and expired results are accepted

For applicants **aged 18 to 54**, IRCC's proof-of-language page (dated 2025-09-11) sets the
requirement at **CLB 4 in speaking and listening**. Read it for who it applies to and what else it
accepts.

Two further points, commonly conflated:

- **Citizenship accepts results that have expired.** IRCC's guide states, of a previous test result,
  "we will accept the previous results even if they have expired." Express Entry, by contrast,
  requires results less than two years old when you complete your profile.
- Paragon separately keeps your score report viewable in your CELPIP account for two years from your
  test date. **That is an account-access window, not an eligibility rule** — two different things
  with the same number attached.

## What is not settled here

- **Provincial Nominee Programs and Quebec.** No IRCC page was found listing PNP or Quebec language
  thresholds, so this page states none. Provinces set their own — check the province.
- **The current fee where you live.** Dated above, and only from the Ontario page.
- **CELPIP-Academic was retired in August 2015** and is not accepted. References still circulate.

## What actually costs you

- Booking LS for an immigration application — IRCC will not accept it, and the fee is gone.
- Booking General for citizenship unchecked — valid, but CAD 95 more and two components nobody asked
  for.
- Studying Reading and Writing for an LS test — weeks aimed at sections you will not sit.
- Assuming a fee you saw on a forum is the fee you will pay in your country.
- Treating Paragon's two-year account window as IRCC's two-year rule.
