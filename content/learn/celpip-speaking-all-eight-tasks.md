## There is no examiner

This is the single most useful thing to know before you prepare, and most people find out on test
day.

**CELPIP is delivered entirely by computer — Speaking included.** You sit at a screen, a prompt
appears, and you speak into a microphone. Your answers are recorded. **Nobody is sitting across the
table from you.**

That changes how you should prepare, in three specific ways:

- **There is no rapport to build.** No smiling, no eye contact, no reading the examiner's face to see
  whether you are doing well. If you have practised for an interview, you have practised for a
  different test.
- **Nobody will help you out.** A human examiner might nod you along, or repeat a question. The screen
  will not. If you stop talking, silence is what gets recorded.
- **The clock does not care.** It starts and it runs. There is no "sorry, could I start again?"

The practical consequence: **practise talking to a device with nobody in the room.** It feels
strange, and that strangeness is exactly what you want to get out of the way before test day rather
than on it.

## The arithmetic nobody puts in front of you

Paragon publishes **15 minutes for the Speaking section, and 8 tasks.**

Divide it. **Under two minutes per task — and that includes your preparation time.**

That single number reorganises your whole preparation. You are not being asked to give a considered,
polished answer. You are being asked to **start talking almost immediately and keep going in a
straight line.** A person who needs thirty seconds to decide what to say has spent a quarter of the
task deciding.

*(Paragon publishes the 15-minute total; it does not publish a per-task breakdown on the pages we
have checked. The division above is arithmetic, not an official split — treat it as the scale of the
problem, not as a stopwatch reading.)*

## All eight tasks

| # | Task | What it asks | Where it is lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **[Giving Advice](/learn/celpip-speaking-task-1-giving-advice)** | Advise a specific person about a specific problem | Generic advice that would suit anyone |
| 2 | **[Talking About a Personal Experience](/learn/celpip-speaking-task-2-personal-experience)** | Tell about something that happened to you | A story with no point — events, no meaning |
| 3 | **[Describing a Scene](/learn/celpip-speaking-task-3-describing-a-scene)** | Describe an image | Listing objects instead of describing a situation |
| 4 | **[Making Predictions](/learn/celpip-speaking-task-4-making-predictions)** | Say what will happen next in that scene | Describing again instead of predicting |
| 5 | **[Comparing and Persuading](/learn/celpip-speaking-task-5-comparing-and-persuading)** | Compare two options, then persuade someone | Comparing and never persuading |
| 6 | **[Dealing with a Difficult Situation](/learn/celpip-speaking-task-6-difficult-situation)** | Choose how to handle an awkward situation and explain | Avoiding the choice |
| 7 | **[Expressing Opinions](/learn/celpip-speaking-task-7-expressing-opinions)** | State an opinion and support it | Stating it and then repeating it |
| 8 | **[Describing an Unusual Situation](/learn/celpip-speaking-task-8-unusual-situation)** | Describe an odd image **to someone who cannot see it** | Forgetting the listener is blind to it |

Read that last column again. **Six of the eight are lost by answering a slightly different question
from the one asked.** Not by weak grammar. Not by accent. By drifting one step off the task.

## What is actually marked

Each CELPIP skill is scored **0 to 12**, and that maps directly to a **CLB level**. Speaking is
marked on how well you communicate — content, coherence, vocabulary, and how easy you are to follow.

**Your accent is not one of the criteria.** Being *understandable* matters; sounding Canadian does
not. Millions of people speak clear, fluent English in an Indian, Nigerian, Filipino or Pakistani
accent, and CELPIP is not marking that.

And the rule that outranks everything else:

> A response giving **insufficient information to assess** scores **0 or 1**.

Talking around the task for ninety seconds without answering it is not a low score. It is the bottom
of the scale.

## Three habits that carry across all eight

1. **Start with the answer.** Not "well, that's an interesting situation…". Say the advice, name the
   choice, state the opinion — in the first sentence. Then spend the rest supporting it.
2. **Keep going.** A slightly clumsy sentence that continues beats a perfect sentence followed by
   four seconds of silence. There is no examiner to fill the gap.
3. **Answer the task you were given.** Re-read that "where it is lost" column. It is the same failure
   eight times over, wearing different clothes.
