## It does not have to be true

Task 2 asks you to talk about a personal experience — a time you helped someone, a difficult
decision, a day that did not go to plan.

**Nobody is checking.** There is no examiner in the room and no way to verify anything you say. You
are not being marked on your life. **You are being marked on how you tell a story in English.**

This matters more than it sounds. Test-takers lose whole seconds — a large fraction of a task that
allows under two minutes including preparation — searching their memory for something that actually
happened and was interesting enough. Meanwhile the clock runs.

**Have two or three flexible stories ready and bend them to fit.** A time something went wrong. A time
you helped someone. A time you changed your mind. Almost any Task 2 prompt can be answered from one
of those three.

## The real failure: events with no point

Here is the answer most people give.

> *"Last year I went to Toronto with my brother. We took the train. It was long. We stayed three
> nights at a hotel near the station. We visited some places and ate at restaurants. The weather was
> cold but it was okay. Then we came back."*

Nothing is wrong with the English. But nothing happened. It is a list of events in the order they
occurred, and it could stop at any point without changing anything.

**A story is not a sequence. A story is a sequence that goes somewhere.** Something has to change —
what you thought, what you did, what you learned, what it cost you.

And if a marker cannot find the point, CELPIP's harshest rule is waiting:

> A response giving **insufficient information to assess** scores **0 or 1**.

## The shape that works

1. **Name the situation in one sentence.** When, who, what.
2. **Get to the problem fast.** The thing that made it worth telling.
3. **What you did about it** — and how you felt while doing it.
4. **How it ended, and what it left you with.** This is the part almost everyone skips, and it is the
   part that turns events into a story.

## A worked example

**The prompt** *(original AlmiWorld practice prompt)*

> Talk about a time you had to help someone you did not know well.

**The response** *(spoken, about 60 seconds)*

> About two years ago I was working an evening shift at a pharmacy, and an older woman came in maybe
> ten minutes before closing. She had a prescription, and it turned out her insurance had lapsed. The
> total came to almost ninety dollars and she just stood there holding her purse.
>
> I did not know her at all. She had never come in while I was on shift.
>
> What I ended up doing was calling the clinic, because I remembered they sometimes had samples of
> that exact medication. It took about twenty minutes and I was well past closing by then. They had
> two weeks' worth. She took the samples and got her insurance sorted the following Monday.
>
> Honestly, the thing that stayed with me was not the phone call. It was that she had come in ten
> minutes before closing — which meant she had probably been working up to it all day, deciding
> whether to ask at all.
>
> Now if someone comes in near closing, I do not sigh. I have thought about that a lot since.

**Why this works.**

- **The problem arrives in the third sentence.** No throat-clearing.
- **It is specific.** Ninety dollars, ten minutes before closing, twenty minutes on the phone, two
  weeks of samples. Specifics are what a general answer cannot fake — and every one of these could be
  invented.
- **Something changed.** The last two lines are the whole reason the story exists. Without them it is
  a pharmacy anecdote; with them it is a story about noticing what it costs someone to ask.
- **The feeling is shown, not announced.** Not *"I felt very sympathetic."* Instead: *"she just stood
  there holding her purse."*

## Two habits worth building

**Prepare situations, not scripts.** A memorised story collapses the moment the prompt is not the one
you memorised for. Three flexible situations, told fresh, survive anything.

**Always land it.** Whatever the prompt, whatever the story, end with what it left you with. If you
practise nothing else for Task 2, practise the last two sentences.
