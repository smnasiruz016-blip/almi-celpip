**Both test makers say neither test is easier, and they are right — but they are answering the wrong
question.** Here is the British Council:

> "There is absolutely no truth in the myth that either test is easier than the other."
> "Neither CELPIP nor IELTS has a set pass score."

IDP, who run IELTS in Canada:

> "CELPIP is not easier or more difficult than IELTS. Each test has advantages that can appeal to a
> test taker's individual strengths or weaknesses."

This is a CELPIP practice site. "CELPIP is easier" is exactly the claim we have an interest in
making — and both of the other side's own organisations contradict it in writing.

The useful question is not which test is easier in the abstract. It is which one suits the person
sitting it. And the biggest difference is not the format or the scoring:

**IELTS Speaking is an 11-to-14-minute face-to-face interview with a human examiner. CELPIP Speaking
is you, a microphone and a countdown, with nobody in the room.**

Some people are better with a person across the table. Some people freeze. That is a real fact about
you, and worth more than any comparison table.

## The failure

The failure is choosing on a prep-blog comparison table — and the tables are stale.
🔴 **IELTS is no longer a paper test.** ielts.org, 5 March 2026:

> "from mid-2026, we will no longer offer IELTS as a paper-based test. All IELTS tests will be
> delivered on computer."

IDP confirms it in Canada: *"IDP no longer offers the IELTS on paper test."* Any page still selling
a paper option, or quoting the old 13-day paper figure, is stale.

CELPIP is *"fully computer delivered"* but *"must be completed in person at one of our test
centres."* There is no at-home option.

## What Canada actually requires

IRCC is specific about versions:

- Express Entry, IELTS: **"You must take the IELTS General Training option."**
- Express Entry, CELPIP: **"You must take the CELPIP-General test."**
- Citizenship has a Listening-and-Speaking-only route on both sides — CELPIP-General LS, or IELTS
  General Training assessed on **Listening 4.5 or higher and Speaking 4.0 or higher**.

Check IRCC's own page for your program before you book anything.

## The formats, side by side

| | CELPIP-General | IELTS General Training |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | 46–55 min, 38 questions, 6 parts | "Approximately 30 minutes" plus 10 minutes of answer-transfer time, 40 questions, 4 parts |
| Reading | 43–56 min, 38 questions, 4 parts | "60 minutes (including transfer time)", 40 questions, 3 sections |
| Writing | 53 min, 2 tasks (email, survey) | "60 minutes", 2 tasks — letter and essay — "BOTH must be completed" |
| Speaking | 15 min, 8 tasks | "11–14 minutes", 3 parts |
| Total | "under 2 hours and 50 minutes", one sitting | "2 hours and 45 mins" |

## The examiner in the room

ielts.org:

> "The Speaking test is a face-to-face interview between the test taker and an examiner. The
> Speaking test is recorded." — "Time allowed: 11–14 minutes"

**"Recorded" means the interview is audio-recorded. It does not mean you speak to a machine.** The
human examiner survives the move to computer delivery — it remains a *"face-to-face session with a
trained IELTS examiner"*.

CELPIP is the opposite arrangement: 8 tasks in 15 minutes, under two minutes each including
preparation, into a microphone with a timer on screen. Nobody rephrases the question when you
stall. **Neither room is harder in general — they are harder for different people, and you
already know which one you are.**

## Two currencies

IELTS reports bands **0 to 9**, in whole and half bands, plus an overall band:

> "The overall band score is the average of the four section band scores rounded to the nearest half
> band."

CELPIP reports **0 to 12, one level per component**, and no overall level. Its score report carries
an Average Score, and Paragon is explicit about what that is not:

> "Average Score is not equivalent to any Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) level"

**Either way, IRCC reads the four skills separately** — the CLB chart is applied skill by skill. For
converting between the tests, see
[the official CELPIP-IELTS concordance, and its own warnings](/learn/celpip-ielts-concordance) — it
carries the study's own caveats, which matter more than its numbers.

## The Listening asymmetry

For CELPIP, IRCC's equivalency chart is **flat: CELPIP N = CLB N, for CLB 4 through 10, identically
across all four skills.** For IELTS it is not flat. IRCC's Federal Skilled Trades table, in IRCC's
own column order:

| CLB | Speaking | Listening | Reading | Writing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 and above | 7.5–9.0 | 8.5–9.0 | 8.0–9.0 | 7.5–9.0 |
| 9 | 7.0 | 8.0 | 7.0 | 7.0 |
| 8 | 6.5 | **7.5** | 6.5 | 6.5 |
| 7 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 |
| 6 | 5.5 | 5.5 | 5.0 | 5.5 |
| 5 | 5.0 | 5.0 | 4.0 | 5.0 |
| 4 | 4.0 | 4.5 | 3.5 | 4.0 |

At CLB 8, IELTS Listening wants **7.5** while the other three want 6.5. At CLB 7, all four are 6.0.

**This does not mean one test is easier.** It means the two tests are not shaped the same way
against CLB, and which one flatters you depends on which skill is your weakest. IRCC's chart stops
at "10 and above" — any table giving IELTS bands for CLB 11 or 12 is not IRCC's.

## Fees and results

Both checked **24 August 2026**, approximate and before tax.

| | CELPIP-General | IELTS General Training |
|---|---|---|
| Fee | CAD 290 + tax (Ontario region page) | ~CAD 361, *"Fee does not include taxes & GST"* |
| Results | 2–4 business days | 1–5 days (computer) |
| Validity for IRCC | Less than 2 years old | Less than 2 years old |

IDP notes fees *"can also vary slightly depending on the test centre, city, and province"*, and
their page carries no last-updated date. **Check the current fee at booking.**

## What actually costs you

- Booking IELTS Academic when the program requires General Training. IRCC's wording is "must".
- Choosing on "which is easier" instead of on whether an examiner across the table helps you or
  wrecks you.
- Averaging in your head. IRCC reads Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking one at a time.
- Assuming the CLB mapping is the same shape for both tests. For CELPIP it is flat; for IELTS,
  Listening sits higher at CLB 8 and 9.
- Planning around a paper IELTS sitting that no longer exists.
- Treating any conversion table as official. IRCC recognises CLB, and only CLB.
