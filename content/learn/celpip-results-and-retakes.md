**There are two different ways to challenge a CELPIP result, and most people have only heard of
one.** You can sit the test again — Paragon publishes one limit, that you may register for "no more
than one Test Session within five (5) calendar days". Or you can ask for a **re-evaluation**, which
is a re-mark of the test you already sat: available within six months, one per component, roughly
one to two weeks, and **the fee is refunded if your Speaking or Writing score goes up.**

That refund condition is the interesting part, and it tells you something. Re-evaluation is aimed at
the human-judged components. It is a different instrument from a retake, and picking the wrong one
costs either money or weeks.

## The failure

The failure is treating "my score was too low" as one problem with one fix. Booking another sitting
when you wanted a second look at your Speaking, and waiting two weeks for a re-mark when you already
know how the sitting went, are different mistakes — and both eat calendar time you may not have.

The second failure is smaller and more common: reading a confident answer about retake limits or
single-skill retakes and planning around it. Much of what circulates here is in no Paragon document.

## When results arrive, and what you actually get

| | What Paragon publishes |
|---|---|
| Availability | "available online through your CELPIP Account in 2-4 business days after your test date" |
| The official document | the PDF score report — it "is official and accepted by institutions" |
| How long you can see it | scores stay viewable in your account for 2 years from your test date |

Source: the CELPIP [test results page](https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/test-results/).

One caution about that first row. **Other official CELPIP pages give different figures for results
timing.** This page uses the dedicated results page rather than trying to reconcile them. If you see
a different figure quoted elsewhere with total confidence, that is why.

The PDF is the thing that matters — it is the document you send. Download it while you can see it.

## Retaking: the one published rule

> "You may register for no more than one Test Session within five (5) calendar days."

That is from Paragon's [policies and forms page](https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/policies-forms/),
and it is the whole of the published limit.

**No lifetime cap and no annual cap is published.** Not "the cap is high" — no cap appears. If
someone gives you a maximum number of attempts, ask which Paragon page says so.

## Re-evaluation: a re-mark, not a retake

Re-evaluation applies to the test you already sat. Paragon publishes these terms:

| | |
|---|---|
| Window to request | within six months |
| How many | one re-evaluation per component |
| Turnaround | approximately 1 to 2 weeks |
| Fee | refunded if your Speaking or Writing score increases |
| Fee amount | **not published on the page checked** |

Two things follow from the shape of that. The refund attaches to Speaking and Writing — the
components a human rates — and not to Listening or Reading. And "one per component" means the door
closes once per section: a re-evaluation of your Writing is a single, unrepeatable request against
that sitting. Because the fee amount is not published, you cannot price the decision from public
information. Ask Paragon before you commit.

## What is not published — and nobody will tell you

This is the part every competing page answers confidently. It should not.

**Whether you can retake a single skill.** No official source says either way. This page will not
claim a single-skill retake exists and will not claim it does not. Both answers circulate and
neither is sourced. **Anyone who tells you confidently is not citing Paragon.** If this is your real
question, it is one for Paragon's support, not for a guide.

**Whether the five-day rule limits registering or sitting.** The published wording is about
registering for a Test Session. Whether it constrains the dates you can sit, as opposed to the dates
on which you can book, is not spelled out.

**Refund and cancellation amounts.** The only fee of this kind found published is a test transfer
fee of **CAD 50 + tax**, as shown on celpip.ca on 24 August 2026. What a cancellation returns, if
anything, is not published in what was checked. Fees change and differ by country — check the
current figure for your region.

**Whether results timing differs by country.** The 2-4 business days figure carries no country
qualification, and no page was found stating whether timing varies by region.

## The three different "two years"

Three separate two-year rules get flattened into one, and they are not the same rule.

| Which two years | Whose rule | What it governs |
|---|---|---|
| Score viewable in your CELPIP Account for 2 years from your test date | Paragon | how long you can see the report in your account |
| Results "less than 2 years old when you complete your Express Entry profile" | IRCC | eligibility for Express Entry, and likewise for the Atlantic, Rural and Home Care pilots |
| No such rule | IRCC, citizenship | expired results are explicitly accepted |

On that last row, the citizenship guide says it plainly:

> "we will accept the previous results even if they have expired"

That line is from IRCC's Guide CIT 0002, and it is the most under-reported fact here: **an expired
CELPIP result can be an eligibility problem for one stream and not for another.** Read the rule
belonging to the program you are applying to, on IRCC's own page, before deciding anything.

Two pages carry that detail rather than repeating it in the margins: which CLB each program asks for
is in **Which CLB you actually need, by program**, and how a CELPIP level maps to a CLB level at all
— including where Paragon and IRCC differ — is in **CELPIP score chart and CLB explained**.

## What actually costs you

- Not downloading the PDF while the report is visible in your account.
- Planning around a lifetime or annual retake cap. None is published.
- Reading Paragon's two-year account window as an IRCC eligibility rule. They are different claims.
- Assuming an expired result is useless. For citizenship, IRCC says otherwise in writing.
- Budgeting a re-evaluation from a fee figure you found on a forum. The amount is not published.
- Treating a confident answer about single-skill retakes as fact. No official source settles it.

This page does not tell you whether to retake or re-evaluate. That decision depends on your
deadline, your program and your own read of the sitting — and on numbers you can only get from
Paragon and IRCC directly.
