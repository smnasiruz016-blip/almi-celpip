## Start with the option you almost chose

**An answer key tells you that you were wrong. It does not tell you what you did.** The option you
nearly chose is the one that shows you your habit — and almost no free CELPIP reading practice
explains the options you did not pick.

So: **four items, one per part, with the passage, every option, and the reasoning for all four** —
*original AlmiWorld practice items*. Answer each before reading on, read every explanation, and note
which trap caught you.

---

## Item 1 — Part 1, Correspondence: a blank from the reply

The blanks are the half nobody practises.

> Hi Tom,
>
> The contractor can repave the driveway on the 14th or the 21st. I'd take the 21st: the 14th is the
> day my mother arrives, and there'll be cars all over the pad. We split the invoice either way, as
> agreed. Both cars must be off the pad by 7 a.m., so one of us parks on Alder Street the night
> before. — Nadia

> Hi Nadia,
>
> Works for me. Let's go with the 21st — I ______. — Tom

**A.** would rather not add to the confusion when your mother arrives
**B.** can't get onto Alder Street before seven
**C.** don't want to cover the whole invoice
**D.** need another week before deciding

**Answer: A** — the only option giving a reason to prefer the 21st over the 14th. **B** borrows real
words but points nowhere: the Alder Street parking applies to either date. **C** contradicts a
settled detail — the invoice is split down the middle. **D** breaks the sentence in front of it,
where Tom has already named the 21st.

**Every option fits the slot. Only one fits the email.**

## Item 2 — Part 2, Apply a Diagram: one table, four different people

The main text **is** the diagram — "point form text and visuals."

**Riverbend Community Centre — Winter Loan Desk**

| Item | Loan | Deposit | Who may borrow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sled | 1 day | none | Library card holders |
| Snowshoes | 3 days | $20 | Members only |
| Skates | 7 days | $15 | Members; non-members with sign-off\* |
| Skis | 3 days | $40 | Members who completed the safety session |

\* Staff sign-off for non-members: **Saturdays only**.

Priya is not a member and never took the safety session; she has a library card. Amit is a member who
took the session; Joel is a member who did not.

**Priya comes to the desk on a Wednesday. Which is true for her?**

**A.** Skis, three days, $40 · **B.** Skates, seven days, $15 · **C.** Sled, one day, no deposit ·
**D.** Snowshoes, three days, $20

**Answer: C** — the only row reaching a non-member with a library card.

Every wrong option reads the table **correctly**, for someone else. **A** is Amit's: skis need
membership *and* the session. **D** fits Amit and Joel both.

**B** is the near miss, true for Joel: skates are open to any member. Priya could get there by
sign-off — but the footnote allows that on Saturdays, and she is there on a Wednesday. **The
footnote is doing all the work.**

## Item 3 — Part 3, Information: when the answer is "not stated"

Part 3 alone offers an option for information that is absent: **"Choose E if the information is not
stated in the reading."** *The A–D letters are mine. Paragon does not publish how the paragraphs are
labelled on screen.*

> **A.** The Marchmont grain elevator, built in 1928, took grain from township farms until the line
> closed in 1994. It stood empty for seventeen years.
>
> **B.** In 2011 it was granted heritage designation. A volunteer group formed that autumn and raised
> money for two years, from a municipal grant and donations by former elevator workers.
>
> **C.** Work began in 2013 and finished three years later than the group had planned, delayed by the
> state of the timber cribbing. It opened as a museum in 2018.
>
> **D.** It now runs summer tours, hosts school visits, and keeps an archive of shipping records. The
> board would like a café.

**Where is this stated? — "The restoration cost more than the group first estimated."**

**A.** Paragraph A · **B.** Paragraph B · **C.** Paragraph C · **D.** Paragraph D ·
**E.** Not stated

**Answer: E.**

**C** is the trap. The work did run long, and delays usually cost money — but the text reports an
overrun in **time** while the statement claims an overrun in **cost**. Part 3 never gives you that
bridge.

**B** attracts you because it is where the money is: a grant, donations, fundraising — none of it an
estimate, or set against a final figure. **A** and **D** make no financial claim; the café is a wish,
not a cost.

**When no paragraph states it, E is the answer, not a last resort.**

## Item 4 — Part 4, Viewpoints: who said it, and how strongly

The two opinion-holders agree more than you expect.

> Councillor Dale Ovenden, who proposed the Sunday closure of Fairlawn Avenue, says it should be
> judged on foot traffic, not complaints: "Merchants counted more people here in June than in any
> June since we began counting."
>
> Rana Whitfield, who chairs the merchants' association, does not dispute the count. "Nobody here
> wants the cars back," she says. "But a six a.m. start means my produce arrives Monday. Move it to
> eleven and I have no objection."

**Which statement best represents Whitfield's view?**

**A.** The pilot should be judged on pedestrian numbers, not complaints.
**B.** The closure is harming local businesses and should be ended.
**C.** The closure is acceptable if the start time moves to allow morning deliveries.
**D.** Merchants have not seen the rise in foot traffic the council claims.

**Answer: C** — it keeps both halves: the closure is fine, six o'clock is not.

**A** is accurate — but it is Ovenden's. **In Viewpoints, a true sentence under the wrong name is the
most common way to lose a mark.** **B** turns her volume up: she wants the cars kept out, and a
later start leaves her with "no objection." A stronger version is still the wrong version. **D** is
false; she "does not dispute the count."

## The four traps, together

- **Part 1 blanks** — grammatical, topical, answering what the email already settled.
- **Part 2** — the diagram read correctly, for the wrong person.
- **Part 3** — nearly stated: right subject, shifted claim. Time for money, plan for outcome.
- **Part 4** — right opinion, wrong name; or right person, overstated.

Four items is a **diagnostic, not practice volume** — it shows which trap you fall into, not how
fast you read. Nor are the conditions real: Reading is timed **per part**, the test "will automatically
move forward to the next part," and **"you can't go back to a previous part."**
